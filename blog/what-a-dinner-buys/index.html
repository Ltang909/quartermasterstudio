What a $35,000 dinner actually buys

September 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Run the numbers in our ROI calculator and you will land near $35,000 as the true cost of a single executive dinner. The surprise, for most teams, is how little of that is the dinner itself.

The event is the smallest line item

The hard event cost, the venue, the food, the beverage, the candles, runs about $13,000 per night for a proper twenty-guest dinner. That is the number everyone budgets for. It is also barely a third of what the evening really costs.

The people are the cost

Somebody has to plan the thing. An in-house events lead spending a quarter of their time on dinners, with salary loaded at 1.25x for benefits and taxes, costs real money every month whether there is an event or not. Add roughly $7,500 a month in operations and $2,100 a month in platform and collateral, and the fully loaded cost of doing it yourself climbs past $35,000 per night. The calculator shows the whole ledger, and the ledger does not flatter the in-house model.

Where the agency model wins

An agency engagement for the same dinner runs about $31,400 all in. The gap is not the catering; it is everything around it. No recruiting cycle, no ramp time, no severance risk, no five-week search when your planner leaves in Q4. You pay for the outcome, a flawless evening, instead of carrying the fixed cost of the capability year round.

There is also the risk line nobody budgets: the likely cost of a hiring misfire on an events role runs near $98,000 once you count recruiting fees, ramp, and replacement. Against that, a $17,000 agency exposure per engagement looks less like a premium and more like insurance.

The dinner is $13,000. The other $22,000 is the price of having someone to plan it. Make sure you are buying the right one.

The one thing money cannot buy

Here is the part no calculator captures: the guest list. Twenty right people in a room is worth more than any line item on the invoice, and it is the one thing neither an in-house team nor an agency can purchase. It has to be built, relationship by relationship, dinner by dinner. That is the strategy. Everything else is provisioning.
