The logbook

The seating chart is strategy, not logistics

September 10, 2026 · 4 min read

The seating chart is the last document we finalize and the first one that decides whether the night works. Who sits next to whom determines what gets said, which is another way of saying it determines what the evening was for.

Three roles at every table

We assign every guest one of three roles before a single name goes on the chart:

  • Anchors are the reason someone is in the room: the prospect, the partner, the decision maker. Anchors get the best conversational real estate, which means seats where they can talk to the most people with the least effort.
  • Bridges are the connectors, the guests who know half the table already and will happily spend the evening making introductions. Every table needs at least two.
  • Sparks are pairings that should happen: two people who do not know each other yet but will have plenty to talk about. The chart manufactures these collisions on purpose.

The rules we never break

Never seat competitors beside each other. Never seat couples together; they can talk at home, and the evening is for new conversations. Alternate host team and guests so no end of the table becomes a company huddle. And the host sits mid-table, never at the head: the head of the table is a podium, and podiums do not have conversations.

Drawn last, not first

We draw the chart after the RSVPs are in, never before. A seating chart built on hoped-for guests is fiction. The real list tells you who the anchors turned out to be, which bridges actually showed up, and where the sparks can fly. Then we revise it twice: once for conversation flow, once while imagining the evening minute by minute.

A beautiful table with a thoughtless seating chart is just expensive catering. The chart is where the strategy lives.

It takes us about three hours to seat twenty guests. That sounds like a lot until you consider that those three hours decide what every guest remembers about the night. We would be glad to spend them on your table.
