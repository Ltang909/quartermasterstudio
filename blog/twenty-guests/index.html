The logbook

Why twenty guests is the magic number

September 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Our signature format is twenty guests. Not nineteen, not twenty-five. Clients sometimes ask if the number is superstition. It is arithmetic.

The math of the room

Twenty guests means 190 possible one-on-one conversations. That is a rich enough network for introductions to spark all evening, but small enough that the room still feels like one event. Push the list to forty and the possible pairings jump past 780. Nobody meets anybody. The evening becomes a reception with chairs.

At twenty, you can still seat everyone at a single table, or two tables of ten that feel like one party. The host can look down the table and see the whole evening at once. That sightline is the difference between hosting and attending your own event.

Every seat is chosen, not filled

A twenty-person list forces curation. There is no room for the plus-ones of obligation, the colleagues who should be invited, the names that fill a ballroom. Each invitation has to earn its place, which means each guest arrives knowing they were wanted there. Guests can feel the difference between a curated room and a filled one within ten minutes.

The host can actually host

Twenty is about the largest number at which a host can still have a genuine exchange with every guest in a single evening. Past that, hosting becomes receiving: a receiving line, a speech, a wave from the podium. The whole point of an executive dinner is the opposite. The host should leave knowing something new about every person who came.

Constraint is what makes the guest list the strategy. Twenty seats means twenty decisions, and every one of them matters.

When to break the rule

Board dinners run smaller, usually twelve to sixteen, because the conversation is the work. Launch celebrations run bigger, forty to sixty, because the energy is the work. But for the classic executive dinner, the one where relationships get built and deals quietly advance, twenty remains the number we provision around.

If you are planning your own table, start with the list before the venue. The venue serves the list, never the other way around. And if you want someone to obsess over the other 189 details, that is what we do.

Twenty seats. Zero compromises.
